Our story

One building.
Bread, then beer.

Number 73 Church Way has fed Iffley for the best part of two centuries. Four chapters — take them at your own pace.

Chapter I · The bakery

Before the beer, bread.

Before it poured anything, 73 Church Way baked. This was Iffley's bakery: the oven lit in the small hours, the village at the door for the morning batch, flour on every surface and drifting out onto the lane.

The walls are thick because they were built to hold heat. A century and a half on, they still do.

Chapter II · The 1880s

A pub is born.

In the 1880s the oven went cold and the handpumps went in. The Prince of Wales opened as the village's own: gaslight, brass, a mahogany bar, and a bench of lock-keepers, college servants and bargemen who knew exactly how they liked their bitter.

It stayed that way for a hundred years, give or take a coat of paint.

Chapter III · 1989 to now

Restored by Sage & Mathew.

Wadworth of Devizes bought the house in 1989, and their ales have been on the handpumps ever since. Sage and Mathew took the place on and run it as their own: they kept the beams, the fireplaces and the timber bar, re-gilded the sign, and made every chair one you'd happily lose an afternoon in.

Old bones, warmed through.

Chapter IV · Today

Five minutes from the water.

Today the Prince is the pub you walk up to from Iffley Lock, where the college eights start their bumps races. Seasonal British cooking, a Sunday roast people cross the city for, Oxfordshire ales on the handpumps, and a garden under the apple trees.

Bread and beer. Come and see.

    The garden & local walks

    Between the village and the river.

    The garden is out the back under the apple trees: sixty of you when it's fine, heaters, blankets and a fire pit when it isn't. Beyond it, five minutes on foot, is the lock, the towpath and the best flat walking in Oxford.

    Pick a walk and it's drawn on the map. Muddy boots and wet dogs are part of the furniture.

      Getting here

      • On foot or by bike — the Thames Path brings you to the lock; up Mill Lane and Church Way and you're here.
      • By bus — the 3 and 3A from the city centre stop at Iffley Turn, five minutes' walk.
      • By car — a little parking on Church Way, more on Iffley Road.
