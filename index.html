Our story

One building.
Three lives.

Number 73 Church Way has been feeding Iffley for the best part of two centuries. Scroll on.

Loaves before pints

Chapter one · The 1800s

The Bakery

Long before anyone pulled a pint here, this was where Iffley got its bread. The building began life as the village bakery — ovens lit before dawn, flour in the air, the smell of the morning batch drifting down Church Way towards the river.

The thick walls that kept the ovens warm are still standing. So, we like to think, is the habit of the village coming through the door for something good.

Gaslight, brass and bitter

Chapter two · The 1880s

A pub is born

By the 1880s the ovens had cooled and the taps had opened. The bakery became the Prince of Wales: a proper village local of dark wood and polished brass, where lock-keepers, college servants and boatmen drank shoulder to shoulder.

It stayed that way for a century, give or take a coat of paint. In 1989 Wadworth, the Devizes brewers, bought the house and kept the ale flowing for another generation.

Chapter three · Independent again

Restored by Sage & Mathew

Then Sage and Mathew took the Prince on as independents. They kept everything worth keeping — the beams, the fireplaces, the timber bar — and added the comfort of a pub you actually want to spend a whole afternoon in.

Proper chairs. A kitchen that cooks with the seasons. Ales from down the road. The kind of welcome that remembers your name the second time.

Five minutes to the water

Chapter four · Now

Today, by the lock

Today the Prince sits a short stroll from Iffley Lock and the Thames. Seasonal British cooking, a Sunday roast people cross the city for, local ales on the handpumps, and a garden that fills the moment the sun appears.

Bread, beer, and a bit of both. Come and see.

    The garden & local walks

    Between the village and the river

    Our garden sits behind the pub under the apple trees — heaters and blankets when it turns, a fire pit for the dark months. Five minutes' walk beyond it is Iffley Lock, the Thames Path and some of the best flat walking in Oxford.
